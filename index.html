~/research

My work sits where high performance computing meets quantum computing. Many quantum workloads, including Hamiltonian simulation, error correction and circuit simulation, come down to manipulating huge numbers of Pauli strings. In my work, I explore how to do that efficiently on modern hardware, including GPUs and distributed-memory clusters. I also work with students on benchmarking real quantum hardware.

./research/

Core of my doctoral thesis

PauLIB

A C++20 library for high-performance processing of large numbers of Pauli strings. It has a CUDA backend for GPUs, an MPI backend for distributed-memory clusters and Python bindings. Its HPC design is the subject of my first-author paper at SC26.

  • C++20
  • CUDA
  • MPI
  • SIMD
  • Python

RepositoryPaper

Benchmark dataset · EQTC26

PRQC Bench

931 peaked random quantum circuits (2–20 qubits, depth 2–50) in OpenQASM 3, used to evaluate the system-level fidelity of quantum computers. An ideal device returns the all-zero bitstring with high probability. The circuits were generated with tensor networks and JAX.

  • OpenQASM 3
  • JAX
  • Benchmarking
